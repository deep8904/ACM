# Design Style

## 1. Direction

The visual identity should feel like a premium independent technology publication: minimal, modern, precise, and editorial.

Avoid copying Apple, The Verge, Wired, or any other publication. Inspiration may come from high-quality editorial systems, but the final language must be distinct.

## 2. Core characteristics

- Strong typography.
- Generous spacing.
- Clean grid.
- Dark and light compatibility.
- Limited accent use.
- Clear information hierarchy.
- Technical detail without visual clutter.
- Consistent image treatment.

## 3. Color starting point

The final palette should be tested for accessibility.

Suggested neutral foundation:

- Near black: `#0B0D10`
- Deep surface: `#14181D`
- Light surface: `#F5F7F9`
- White: `#FFFFFF`
- Muted text: `#68717C`

Suggested accent starting point:

- Electric blue: `#4C7DFF`

Do not use the accent on every element.

## 4. Typography

Use a modern sans-serif with strong screen readability. Geist or Inter are suitable implementation options. Use a monospace face only for code, technical labels, and small metadata.

Hierarchy:

- Hero headline: bold, compact, high contrast.
- Article headline: strong but not oversized.
- Body: comfortable width and line height.
- Captions and metadata: small but accessible.

## 5. Hero images

Preferred approaches:

- Editorial product composition.
- Abstract visual representing the underlying technology.
- Clean diagrams.
- Licensed press imagery with clear provenance.
- Purpose-built 3D or generated art that is labeled and does not misrepresent reality.

Avoid:

- Random glowing circuits.
- Humanoid robots for every AI story.
- Excessive neon.
- Fake interfaces.
- Text baked into hero images unless part of a designed social graphic.
- Generated product photos that could be mistaken for official hardware.

## 6. Social graphics

### LinkedIn

- One strong statement or one key visual.
- Minimal copy.
- Editorial diagram or product crop.
- Clear brand mark.
- Readable at feed size.

### Instagram carousel

- Slide 1: concise hook.
- Slides 2-6: one idea per slide.
- Final slide: practical conclusion and article direction.
- Consistent margins and type scale.
- Avoid dense paragraphs.

### X

Usually use the article hero or a simple evidence graphic.

## 7. Image prompt rules

Every image-generation prompt should define:

- Editorial purpose.
- Subject.
- Composition.
- Lighting.
- Materials.
- Background.
- Camera or rendering language where relevant.
- Aspect ratio.
- Excluded visual clichés.
- Whether text must be absent.

## 8. Accessibility

- Minimum WCAG AA contrast for text.
- Alt text should describe function and meaning.
- Do not rely only on color.
- Avoid small text in social images.
- Maintain safe margins for platform crops.

Milestone 8 visual briefs and image prompts are text-only. Prefer official assets or clearly abstract/editorial/diagrammatic concepts; prohibit fake official screenshots, fabricated unreleased hardware, unauthorized logos, implied hands-on possession, and misleading product renders. Every carousel slide and visual concept requires useful alt text and an explicit misinformation-risk note.
