# Audience

## Primary audience

Deep writes for technically curious readers who want useful explanations of new technology without corporate marketing language.

Core segments:

- Developers and software engineering students.
- UI/UX and product designers.
- Game developers and serious gaming audiences.
- Creators choosing tools and hardware.
- Technology buyers who value practical tradeoffs.

## Reader needs

Readers want to know:

- What changed?
- Why does it matter?
- Is the announcement meaningful or mostly marketing?
- Who benefits?
- What are the limitations?
- Should they try, buy, upgrade, wait, or ignore it?
- What does it mean for developers, designers, creators, or gamers?

## Knowledge assumptions

Assume familiarity with common technology terms, but explain specialized concepts briefly. Do not talk down to beginners. Use examples when a technical detail affects a practical decision.

## Geographic posture

The publication can serve a global English-speaking audience while acknowledging U.S. pricing and availability when relevant. Clearly label region-specific information.

## Relationship with readers

The voice should feel like a knowledgeable peer who has done the research, not a salesperson, lecturer, or anonymous news wire.
